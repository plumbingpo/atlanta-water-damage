Few emergencies in a building are as hazardous as sewage backing up into it. Raw sewage contains dangerous bacteria, viruses, parasites and other pathogens, including E. coli, hepatitis, salmonella and giardia, that pose serious health risks to anyone who touches contaminated materials. Atlanta Restoration Co provides emergency sewage cleanup throughout Atlanta, responding 24/7 with properly trained technicians, appropriate personal protective equipment and EPA-registered disinfectants to decontaminate your property safely.

Sewage damage is classified as Category 3 (black water), the most contaminated and dangerous category of water damage. Because of it, cleanup follows the toughest rules in the trade, which means full protective gear, barriers to contain the area, tearing out any porous material that met sewage and disinfecting all that is left. We work to the IICRC S500 standard that governs Category 3 losses, with the goal of returning your property to a condition safe to occupy.

The cleanup process involves removing all standing sewage, extracting contaminated water from structural materials, removing and properly disposing of every porous material that contacted sewage (carpet, padding, drywall, insulation), thoroughly disinfecting hard surfaces, applying antimicrobial treatments and completing structural drying to prevent secondary mold growth. We also deodorize the affected area with industrial-grade hydroxyl generators and thermal fogging equipment to eliminate lingering sewage odors.

How to Tell You Need Sewage Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • A rotten egg or sewer smell from the drains or the lowest floor of the home
  • Gurgling sounds from toilets or drains when other fixtures are used
  • Dark residue from sewage left on the basement floor or ringing a drain
  • Dark, dirty water or sewage rising out of a tub, toilet or floor drain
  • Every sink, tub and shower draining slowly even after you plunge them
  • Toilet water with waste in it soaking into carpet or running across the floor
  • Damp patches on a wall or floor that also smell foul

What a Professional Brings to Sewage Cleanup

Sewage cleanup is not something any homeowner should attempt. The biological hazards in raw sewage require specialized training, proper personal protective equipment (Tyvek suits, respirators, protective gloves and boots) and EPA-registered disinfection products that are not available to consumers. People exposed to the pathogens in sewage can become seriously ill with skin and respiratory infections, hepatitis or gastrointestinal disease.

Contaminated materials count as biohazard waste, and disposing of them properly is part of a professional cleanup. Putting carpet, insulation or drywall soaked with sewage out with household trash breaks the law and endangers sanitation workers and your community. Licensed biohazard disposal facilities take this waste from certified restoration firms, which keep the required protocols in place.

Maintenance Tips for Sewage Cleanup

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Protect against municipal sewer backups by having a backwater valve fitted where your line connects
  • Every 2 to 3 years, get a sewer camera run through the line to spot developing issues
  • Never pour grease, oil or food waste down your drains, since these cause blockages
  • Keep downspouts and sump pump discharge out of the sanitary sewer so it does not overload in heavy rain
  • Fit a strainer over each sink and shower drain so hair and debris stay out of the pipes
  • Find your sewer cleanout now and check its condition, so it can be reached in an emergency
